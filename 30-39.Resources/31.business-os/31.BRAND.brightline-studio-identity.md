# Brightline Studio (Brand Identity)

## Voice

- **Direct** — says what something costs and what it involves up front; never buries the price three scrolls down.
- **Warm, not precious** — friendly like a good colleague, not "wellness-brand" soft-focus language.
- **Specific** — "a 30-minute recipe blog post" beats "delicious home cooking content" every time; concrete details are the brand's actual style, on the page and in the photos.

## Tone do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Name the exact deliverable ("10 styled photos, one plating guide") | Vague value language ("elevate your brand's visual story") |
| Use short, plain sentences | Stack adjectives ("stunning, breathtaking, unforgettable") |
| Admit a limitation plainly ("this won't work for dark, moody plating") | Oversell scope to close a sale |

## Visual notes

Warm, natural-light photography (no heavy filters); a simple two-color palette (a warm off-white background, one accent color per season's shoots) so a feed of otherwise-varied client work still reads as one studio's output.

## Sample line

"Ten photos, one afternoon, no reshoots — here's exactly what that looks like before you book."

## Notes

This is a fictional brand used throughout this template's worked examples. Because Brightline Studio is a single-person business, its Company and Brand are effectively the same voice — most solo operators won't need to separate these two files' content much further than this.
