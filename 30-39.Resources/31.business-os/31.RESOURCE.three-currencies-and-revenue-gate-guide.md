# Three Currencies and the Revenue Gate

> A short operating model for running a side business alongside a job, without either one quietly eating the other. Part of the **Business OS** module — see `MODULES.md` at the repository root for what else this module includes.

## Why this exists

Most task systems track one thing: is it done or not. That works fine for a single project, but it breaks down the moment you're juggling a day job, a side business, and a life outside both — because "I did a lot of tasks this week" can still mean you built the wrong thing, or built the right thing at the cost of everything else. This guide adds two small, deliberately simple tools on top of the base system: a way to notice *which kind of progress* a piece of work actually produces, and a rule for deciding what to start next.

## The three currencies

Everything you spend an evening or a weekend on produces (at most) one of these. Naming which one, honestly, is the whole exercise — most stalled side businesses aren't short on effort, they're short on effort aimed at the currency that actually needed it.

| Currency | What it is | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Money** | A direct revenue event — something a real customer paid for, or a concrete step toward one paying | A sale, an invoice sent, a paying signup, a client proposal sent |
| **Capital** | Skill, reputation, or relationships that will compound into future earning power, even though nothing was paid today | Shipping a portfolio piece, learning a tool your business needs, a relationship that could become a referral later |
| **Wellbeing** | The health, rest, and relationships that fund your ability to do the other two at all | Sleep, exercise, time with family, a hobby that isn't "content" |

**The rule of thumb:** before you start a task, name which currency it's for. If you can't name one, it's probably admin — necessary sometimes, but don't count it as "building the business" when you're deciding what to work on next. And don't let Wellbeing quietly become the currency that always loses the argument — a side business that burns out its owner has produced negative Capital, not just zero.

## The revenue gate (for Money-currency work specifically)

A revenue gate is one frontmatter field, added to a `.PRJ.` note, that answers a single question honestly: *does this project have a path to a paying customer, or doesn't it?*

```yaml
Revenue Path: "Direct" | "Enabling" | "None"
```

| Value | Meaning | Priority rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | This project's own output is sold to a customer (or is being actively pitched to one right now) | Serve these first |
| **Enabling** | This project doesn't sell anything itself, but a Direct project genuinely can't ship without it (infrastructure, a required tool, a foundational system) | Serve these second |
| **None** | Not yet validated — an idea, a hobby, an experiment with no identified buyer yet | Frozen: no more building time until it either earns a Direct path or you deliberately choose to spend an evening exploring it |

**The one hard rule:** don't start a brand-new `None` project while an existing `Direct` project still has zero paying customers. This isn't a ban on ideas — see the ideas-inventory pattern below for where new ideas go instead of straight into a project file. It's a ban on *building* a second unproven thing before the first one has been offered to a real buyer at least once.

A `None` project is not a failure — it's honestly labeled. Calling something a hobby and meaning it (no guilt, no pressure to monetize it) is often the healthier outcome than quietly abandoning it while still calling it "the business."

## How this connects to the rest of the system

- **GOAL work streams** (see the base `.GOAL.` template) are where you sequence Direct and Enabling projects into a period's priority order — the revenue gate tells you *which* projects are even eligible to be prioritized; the GOAL file's work streams tell you the order.
- **Component files** (`.COMPANY.`, `.BRAND.`, `.PRODUCT.` — templates `10`–`12`) describe the business and its offerings once, so every Direct project can reference them instead of re-explaining the same positioning in every project note. Worked examples: `31.COMPANY.brightline-studio-foundation.md`, `31.BRAND.brightline-studio-identity.md`, `31.PRODUCT.styled-recipe-shot-list-guide.md` (all in this folder).
- **The ideas-inventory pattern** (see `20-29.Areas/21.active_areas/21.AREA.ideas-inventory.md`) is where a `None`-stage idea lives *before* it becomes a project — so "I have an idea" and "I'm now building it" stay visibly different states.
- **North Star & Waypoints, and the weekly scoreboard** (Steering Pack module, if you have it — see `32.steering-pack/32.RESOURCE.north-star-waypoints-and-scoreboard-guide.md`) sit one layer above this: this guide is about *what to build next*; that one is about *whether it's adding up to anything over a year or more*.

## Notes on using this pattern

- Add the `Revenue Path` field only to `.PRJ.` notes that are actually business-related — a personal side project with no intent to ever sell it doesn't need this field at all; don't force the framework onto everything.
- Re-label a project's `Revenue Path` the moment its reality changes — the field is a live signal, not a decision you make once and forget. A `None` idea that lands its first paying customer becomes `Direct` that same day.
- This is a worked, fictional example: see `10-19.Projects/11.active_projects/11.PRJ.launch-styled-recipe-shot-list-product.md` for a `.PRJ.` note using `Revenue Path: "Direct"` in practice, sourced from the ideas-inventory example below it.
