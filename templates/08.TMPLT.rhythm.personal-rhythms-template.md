# Template: Personal Rhythms

> A short, standing list of the recurring habits and practices you want to protect — the things you do *regardless* of what's on your project list that week. This is deliberately not a task list.

## What this template is for

Most of what's in this vault is *work*: projects with a finish line, areas with a standard to maintain, tasks you check off. A Rhythms note is different — it's for the handful of recurring personal practices (a weekly planning walk, a no-meetings block, an offline evening, a regular check-in with a friend or family member, a exercise habit) that keep you functioning well enough to do the work at all. Think of it as a short reference card you glance at, not a checklist you clear.

**This template is intentionally generic and non-prescriptive.** Fill it with whatever rhythms matter to *you* — the worked example below uses secular, work/life-balance rhythms as an illustration, but the same template works equally well for a fitness routine, a spiritual practice, a creative habit, or anything else you want to protect on a recurring basis.

## Required frontmatter fields

```yaml
---
Type: "Rhythm"
Review Frequency: "<how often you re-check this list — monthly is typical>"
---
```

## Required sections

1. `## Rhythms I'm protecting` — a short list (aim for 3–7, not 20) of the recurring practices themselves, with roughly how often each happens.
2. `## Why these` — one or two sentences on what these rhythms are for — what breaks down if they slip.
3. `## Notes` — optional: adjustments over time, what's working, what isn't.

## Notes on using this template

- **Never turn rhythm items into `- [ ]` tasks.** The whole point of this note type is that these are practices you return to, not a backlog that accumulates guilt when unchecked. If you find yourself scoring or tracking completion of these day to day, you've turned it back into a task list — pull it back out.
- A missed rhythm is worth noticing, not worth punishing. If you skip your planning walk for two weeks, that's useful information about what's crowding it out — write one factual line in `## Notes` if you want, and move on. This note observes, it doesn't nag.
- This lives in the Areas range (no finish line, ongoing) but uses its own `.RHYTHM.` marker rather than `.AREA.`, because it deliberately skips the `## Todo` section every other Area file has — there is no todo list for a rhythm.
- Review this list roughly monthly. Life changes; a rhythm that mattered six months ago might not fit anymore, and that's fine — update the list rather than treating it as fixed forever.
